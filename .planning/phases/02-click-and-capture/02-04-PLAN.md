## Phase Goal

**As a** pianist drilling a passage alone, **I want to** play it many times to a metronome and have every repetition recorded as raw MIDI that nothing can lose, **so that** later phases can tell me what I keep getting wrong from data I trust.

<objective>
Close the phase the way the project constraint demands: re-run every automated gate fresh (unit suite, run-path gate, the Phase 1 notehead map check, the headless capture round trip), give the user a plain checklist to keep open at the piano, and then stop for the two real checks at the Roland FP-60X — first the clock relationship (single notes on the click, median near zero, no drift with the tab in the background), then a 10+ pass drill on a ladder file that comes back complete after the tab is closed.

Purpose: ROADMAP success criteria 2 and 5 are the only proof that the MIDI-clock versus audio-clock relationship holds on the real hardware and that a real drill is captured with nothing missing. The headless checks make sure the piano session is spent on those judgements, not on a software defect.

Output: README.md and fixtures/README.md updated, all gates green, and the user's two checkpoint responses recorded verbatim in the SUMMARY.
</objective>

The collapsible "Clock readout" shows the last note's offset from the nearest click (positive = late), the median over the last 20 notes, the count, and the AudioContext base and output latency.   <how-to-verify>
Sit at the FP-60X with the laptop connected over USB and fixtures/README.md open at "Phase 2 - click and capture at the piano".

1. Double-click index.html. When Chrome asks to use MIDI, click Allow. The MIDI input list must show the FP-60X and the state must read `Connected: <its name>`. If the list is empty, follow the "input not found" steps in the checklist and note what was needed.
2. Open fixtures/01-right-hand.musicxml. Set BPM to 80. Press Start. You must hear a steady click with a higher-pitched accent every 5th click (rung 1 is 5/4). The state line reads `Recording session <n> at 80 BPM - pass 1`.
3. Expand "Clock readout". Play C4 exactly on the click, 8 times in a row, listening rather than watching. Read: the last-note offset (jumps around by a few tens of ms — normal), the median of the last 20, the count (8), and the two latency numbers. Write the median down. Expected: within about ±25 ms of 0. A larger steady value (for example +40 ms every time) is a finding, not a mistake — write it down as is.
4. Change BPM to 120 in the field while the session runs. The click must speed up without stopping and the session number must not change. Play 8 more notes on the click; the median should stay near the value you wrote down.
5. Switch to another window and leave Chrome in the background for three full minutes while the click keeps going (put a phone metronome next to it at the same BPM if you want a reference). Come back: the click must still be steady and in step with where it was; play 8 more notes on the click and the median must not have moved by more than about 10 ms from step 3.
6. Press Stop. Fully quit Chrome (all windows). Reopen index.html by double-click. Note whether Chrome asks for MIDI permission again (either answer is fine; the answer settles research assumption A1) and confirm the FP-60X is listed as Connected again.
